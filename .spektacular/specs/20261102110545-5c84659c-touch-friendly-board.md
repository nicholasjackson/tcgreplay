# Feature: 20261102110545-5c84659c-touch-friendly-board

## Overview

Players can follow, play and review games on the board from an iPad or an iPhone using touch alone, without a mouse or trackpad. Today the board responds only to a mouse: cards can't be moved, menus can't be opened and numbers can't be entered by touch, and the board doesn't fit a phone's screen. This brings drag and drop, card actions sized for each screen, a way to pick targets by tapping the board, and a phone-sized board, so any device in the player's hand works as a playmat, while mouse use on the desktop keeps working as it does now.

## Requirements

- [ ] **Move cards by touch**
  Players can drag a face-up card with a finger to any place they can drop it with a mouse today. Each drop has the same result as the matching mouse drop.
- [ ] **Scroll the hand without picking up a card**
  Players can swipe sideways through a hand that is wider than the screen without lifting a card. Moving a card up out of the hand picks it up.
- [ ] **See where a card can go while dragging**
  While a card is being dragged, every place it can be dropped is marked and the place under the pointer is highlighted. Letting go anywhere else leaves the card where it was.
- [ ] **Open a card's actions by touch**
  Players can tap any card that has actions today, in hand, in play, attached to a Pokémon, in the deck or in a pile, and reach every action the mouse menu offers for it. Two things change:
  - the card is shown with its text alongside its actions, which replaces today's separate "Zoom" menu item;
  - actions listed once per target ("Attach to Bench 1", "Attach to Bench 2", …) become one action with the target chosen on the board.
- [ ] **Actions fit the screen they are shown on**
  On a phone, a card's actions fill the screen, each group of actions on one row that scrolls sideways when it overflows. On a tablet, the card is shown beside its actions, and a long list of actions scrolls on its own.
- [ ] **Mouse behaviour stays familiar**
  With a mouse, hovering over a card still enlarges it, clicking a card still shows it enlarged with its text, right-clicking still opens its actions beside the pointer (now grouped, as on touch), and dragging still moves it. On a device with both touch and a trackpad, each input behaves this way as it is used.
- [ ] **Actions are grouped**
  A card's actions are grouped by purpose, with the common actions first and destructive actions such as Knock Out last. The groups for each kind of card, and their order, are those set by the design named in Constraints. A group with no actions is not shown.
- [ ] **Choose a target by tapping the board**
  For any action that needs a destination (attaching, evolving, retreating, switching, moving an attached card, choosing an attack's target), players choose it by tapping it on the board rather than from a list. The board shows which places they can choose and what the action is, and they can cancel without anything happening.
- [ ] **One possible target acts straight away**
  When an action has exactly one possible target, choosing the action applies it to that target at once, and the action's label names the target.
- [ ] **Set damage without typing in a dialog**
  Players can raise, lower or set the damage on a Pokémon from its actions, without a browser dialog.
- [ ] **Toggle Special Conditions in one tap each**
  Players can turn each Special Condition on an Active Pokémon on or off with a single tap, and can see which are on.
- [ ] **Attack without browser dialogs**
  Players can choose an attack, adjust its damage for Weakness and Resistance, choose its target (defaulting to the opponent's Active) and confirm, all within the card's actions. The confirm control states the target and the damage.
- [ ] **Draw or look at several cards without browser dialogs**
  Players can choose how many cards to draw, or how many to look at from the top of the deck, within the deck's actions. The count cannot exceed the cards in the deck.
- [ ] **The board fits a phone held upright**
  On a phone in portrait, the whole board fits the screen width without sideways page scrolling. Cards are large enough to read and tap. Both players' piles and prize counts stay visible and can be opened, and a full bench of 8 can be reached.
- [ ] **A phone held sideways asks to be turned**
  On a phone in landscape, the board asks the player to turn the phone upright instead of showing an unusable board.
- [ ] **Everything stays reachable on a phone**
  Every control available on the desktop board (branch, review mode, reveal all, the timeline, deck lists, account actions) can still be reached on a phone.
- [ ] **Controls are large enough to tap**
  On a touch screen, every control and card target is at least 44×44 CSS pixels.
- [ ] **Touch does not trigger the device's own gestures**
  Touching and holding a card does not select text or open the device's callout or preview menu.
- [ ] **Touch behaviour is covered by automated tests**
  Automated tests exercise the board by touch on phone- and tablet-sized screens, and by mouse on a desktop-sized screen, and run as part of the project's checks.

## Constraints

- The board must be built to the design document "Touch-friendly board": `mobile-board/design.md` in the project's `design` source, read with `spektacular design read`, with its mockups at `mobile-board/mockups.html` in the same source. The design governs the detail. Where it and this spec disagree, raise it with the user rather than choosing. The design settles the touch interaction model, target selection, the grouped menu model and its sections for each menu, the three containers (phone sheet, tablet panel, desktop popover), the panels that replace browser prompts, and the compact phone layout.
- One build of the board must serve phones, tablets and desktops, on both the desktop app and the hosted playmat. There is no separate mobile app or mobile site.
- The board must stay a playmat, not a rules engine. Target selection offers the candidates the current menus offer and enforces no game rules.
- The board must handle a bench of up to 8 Pokémon (Area Zero Underdepths), the limit the playmat now allows.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] **Hand to bench by touch**
  On a touch-emulated phone and tablet, dragging a Pokémon from the hand onto an empty bench slot puts it on the bench, and the board and timeline show the same move a mouse drag would produce.
- [ ] **Every drop target accepts a touch drop**
  By touch, a card can be dropped on the Active Spot, a bench slot, an in-play Pokémon (to attach), the discard pile, the deck, the Lost Zone, the Stadium, the play area and the hand. Each drop has the same result as the same drop with a mouse.
- [ ] **A sideways swipe scrolls the hand**
  On a phone whose hand overflows the screen, a horizontal swipe across the hand scrolls it and leaves every card in the hand. An upward drag from a card in the hand picks that card up.
- [ ] **Drop targets are marked and a missed drop is undone**
  While a card is being dragged, every valid drop target is visibly marked and the one under the pointer is highlighted. Releasing over no target leaves the card where it started, with nothing added to the timeline.
- [ ] **A tap opens the card's actions**
  On a touch-emulated phone or tablet, tapping a card in hand, an in-play Pokémon, an attached card, the deck, and a card in a pile view each opens actions for that card, showing the card and offering its text. The actions offered match what a right-click offers for the same card on the desktop, apart from the two changes in "Open a card's actions by touch": the "Zoom" item is replaced by the card shown with its text, and per-target items become one action with the target chosen on the board.
- [ ] **The phone shows actions full screen with rows that scroll sideways**
  On a 390×844 touch screen, a card's actions cover the whole screen. A group whose actions are wider than the screen scrolls sideways and shows that more actions are off screen.
- [ ] **The tablet shows the card beside its actions**
  On a 1024×768 touch screen, the card is shown on the left and its actions on the right. When the actions are taller than the panel, only the actions scroll and the card stays in view.
- [ ] **Mouse hover, click, right-click and drag still work**
  On a desktop-sized screen with a mouse, hovering over a face-up card enlarges it, clicking it shows it enlarged with its text, right-clicking opens its grouped actions at the pointer, and dragging moves it. On a screen with both touch and a mouse, a tap opens the actions and a click shows the card enlarged.
- [ ] **Actions appear in groups, empty groups hidden**
  The actions for an Active Pokémon with two attacks and no Ability appear under group headings, with attacks first and Knock Out last, and no Ability group is shown.
- [ ] **Attaching an Energy by tapping a Pokémon**
  Choosing Attach for an Energy in hand closes the actions, shows a prompt naming the Energy, and highlights every Pokémon the player has in play. Tapping one attaches the Energy to it. Cancelling, or tapping somewhere other than a highlighted Pokémon, attaches nothing and adds nothing to the timeline.
- [ ] **Every destination action uses board selection**
  Evolve, Retreat, Switch, moving an attached card and changing an attack's target each let the player pick the destination by tapping the board, offering the same candidates the current menus list.
- [ ] **A single target applies at once**
  With exactly one Pokémon on the bench, the Active Pokémon's Retreat action is labelled with that Pokémon's name and retreats to it as soon as it is chosen, without board selection.
- [ ] **Damage changes with no dialog**
  From a Pokémon's actions, the player can add 10, 30 or 50 damage, remove 10, and type a total. The damage shown on the board changes to match, and no browser dialog appears at any point.
- [ ] **Conditions toggle in one tap and show their state**
  Tapping Paralyzed on an Active Pokémon's actions marks it Paralyzed on the board and shows the toggle as on. Tapping it again clears it.
- [ ] **An attack completes with no dialog**
  Choosing an attack shows its printed damage. The player can change the damage, keep the default target (the opponent's Active) or pick another opposing Pokémon on the board, and confirm with a control naming the target and damage. The timeline then records that attack, damage and target, and no browser dialog appears.
- [ ] **Draw N and Look at top N with no dialog**
  From the deck's actions, the player can draw 3 cards, and look at the top 4, by picking the number on screen. The count cannot be set above the cards left in the deck, and no browser dialog appears.
- [ ] **Portrait phone board fits**
  On a 390×844 screen in portrait, the page has no horizontal scrollbar. Both players' deck, discard, Lost Zone and prize counts are visible. Tapping each opens that pile or view. All 8 Pokémon on a full bench can be reached by scrolling the bench row.
- [ ] **Landscape phone shows the turn notice**
  On an 844×390 phone screen in landscape, the board is replaced by a notice asking the player to turn the phone upright. Turning it upright shows the board again with nothing lost.
- [ ] **Header controls reachable on a phone**
  On a 390×844 screen, the branch selector, review mode, reveal all, the timeline, the deck list and (hosted) sign out can each be reached and used.
- [ ] **Tap targets measure at least 44×44 CSS pixels**
  On a touch-emulated phone and tablet, every button, toggle and card target the board shows measures at least 44×44 CSS pixels.
- [ ] **Holding a card triggers no device gesture**
  On an iPad or iPhone, touching and holding a card for two seconds neither selects text nor opens the system callout or preview.
- [ ] **Automated touch and mouse tests run with the project's checks**
  Running the project's test command runs browser tests by touch at phone and tablet sizes and by mouse at desktop size. Those tests cover a touch drag from hand to bench, attaching through board selection, the damage controls, a full attack, and the desktop right-click actions, and they pass.

## Technical Approach

- **Pointer Events for every input.** Replace HTML5 drag and drop, the right-click handler and hover previews with Pointer Events. Gestures behave according to each event's pointer type rather than a guess about the device, so one code path serves mouse, pen and touch.
- **The menu builders describe grouped actions; containers present them.** Each menu is built once as grouped actions. A single renderer shows it as the phone sheet, the tablet panel or the desktop popover, so the sections and their controls are written once.
- **The work is web-only.** The change is expected to stay inside the web board, with no server change needed.
- **Suggested order:** dragging and target selection first (this alone makes the iPad usable), then the grouped menus and containers, then the panels that retire browser prompts, then the compact phone layout, with automated touch tests alongside.
- **Browser tests with Playwright.** Add Playwright to the web board as a dev dependency, using its phone and tablet touch profiles and a desktop mouse profile, wired into the project's test command. The repo has no frontend tests today, so this sets up the first suite.
- **Risks.**
  - iOS Safari's own gestures (callouts, text selection, scroll bounce) can fight custom touch handling.
  - Telling a scroll from a drag in the hand needs tuning on real devices.
  - Touch emulation in tests may not reproduce every iOS behaviour, so a manual check on a real iPad and iPhone is still needed.

## Success Metrics

- The player plays a full play-mode game against the assistant on an iPad using touch alone, without a keyboard or trackpad.
- The player follows and acts in a game on an iPhone in portrait without zooming the page or scrolling it sideways.
- No action on the board opens a browser dialog on any device.
- Every action the desktop menus offer today can be reached by touch on both an iPad and an iPhone. None is lost in the move to grouped actions; the "Zoom" item's job is done by the card shown with its actions.
- Attaching an Energy to a benched Pokémon takes at most three taps (the Energy in the hand, Attach, then the Pokémon), however many Pokémon are in play.
- The automated touch and mouse tests pass on every run of the project's test command.

## Non-Goals

- Long-press gestures.
- Installing the board as a home-screen web app.
- Changes to the assistant's side: the MCP tools, the coaching guides and the game model are unchanged.
- New card actions. This spec changes how existing actions are reached, not which actions exist.
- A visual redesign of the desktop board beyond the grouped popover and target selection.
