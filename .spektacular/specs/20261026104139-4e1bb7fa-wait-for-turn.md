## Overview

When the player plays against the assistant on the playmat, or plays a turn the assistant has handed them in a review, the assistant waits while the player takes their turn on the board and then picks up its own part by itself: its next turn, or a response the player's move calls for, such as promoting after a knockout. Today the player has to type "your turn" in chat every time, which breaks the feel of a real match. Players practising on the playmat get a game that flows like a real one, with the board showing when the opponent is waiting or acting, and a simple "Still playing?" check if they step away.

## Requirements

- [ ] **Assistant can wait for the player's turn**
  Whenever the assistant has handed the player a turn to play on the board, it can wait for that turn to finish without the player typing anything in chat. In play mode this is every turn of the player's seat. In review mode it is a turn the assistant handed over in a replay drill or while playing a line out.
- [ ] **The wait is tied to the handed-over seat**
  In review mode, where neither seat belongs to anyone, the wait is tied to the seat whose turn was handed to the player, and only that seat ending its turn counts as the player finishing.
- [ ] **Turn hand-off on end of turn**
  When the player ends their turn on the board, the assistant's wait ends and the assistant learns it is now its turn, along with what the player did during their turn.
- [ ] **Mid-turn promotion**
  When the player knocks out the assistant's Active Pokémon during the player's turn, whether by attack, ability or any other effect, the assistant's wait ends so it can promote a new Active Pokémon.
- [ ] **Responding to the player's card effects**
  When the player plays a card or uses an effect during their turn that requires the assistant's side to do something (for example Judge or Unfair Stamp, where the opponent shuffles their hand into their deck and draws, or an effect where the opponent chooses), the assistant's wait ends so it can carry out its side's part straight away, during the player's turn.
- [ ] **Assistant judges what its side must do**
  The assistant decides from the card's text whether its side has anything to do and what, so a card the assistant has not seen before is handled the same way as a familiar one.
- [ ] **Board locks while the opponent acts**
  In play mode, and in review mode while a turn is handed over (a replay drill or a line being played out), the player cannot take game actions on the board while the assistant is acting for its seat, whether on its own turn or in a mid-turn response, and the board shows that the opponent is acting. The player can still look at the board. The board unlocks as soon as the assistant has finished. The rest of review mode is never locked.
- [ ] **The lock cannot stick**
  If the assistant makes no move for 2 minutes (by default) while the board is locked, for example because the session dropped, the board unlocks by itself and shows the player that the opponent stopped responding. The time counts from the assistant's last move, so a long turn in which it keeps moving is not cut off.
- [ ] **Waiting resumes after a mid-turn response**
  After promoting or carrying out its part of an effect mid-turn, the assistant goes back to waiting, because the player's turn continues until the player ends it.
- [ ] **Waiting lasts as long as the turn**
  The assistant keeps waiting for as long as the player's turn is in progress, without posting anything in chat, until the turn ends, something needs the assistant, or the inactivity check-in fires.
- [ ] **Five-minute inactivity check-in**
  If the player has done nothing on the board for five minutes (by default) while the assistant is waiting, the assistant stops waiting and asks the player in chat, simply, "Still playing?".
- [ ] **Activity restarts the five minutes**
  Every action the player takes on the board restarts the five minutes, so a long but active turn is never interrupted.
- [ ] **Resume after check-in**
  When the player answers the check-in, the assistant resumes waiting with a fresh five minutes. If the player ended their turn while away, the assistant takes its turn instead.
- [ ] **Wait ends when the game changes**
  The wait ends promptly, with the reason, if the game's mode is switched, the game is replaced by another game, closed or expired for inactivity on the hosted server, or the board moves to a different branch or point in the game (a branch switch or a rewind). The assistant then stops waiting rather than hanging, or waiting on the wrong line.
- [ ] **Waiting does not keep a game alive**
  On the hosted server, an assistant that is only waiting does not count as activity on the game, so an abandoned game still expires as it does today.
- [ ] **Board shows the assistant is waiting**
  While the assistant is waiting for the player's turn, the board shows "Opponent is waiting" to the player. The message goes away once the assistant is no longer waiting, including after the check-in.
- [ ] **Assistant knows the play loop**
  Without being told in the session, the assistant knows the loop: after handing the player a turn (ending its own turn in play mode, or setting the player a drill or a line to play out in review mode), it waits for the player, acts on what the wait reports, never asks the player to say "your turn" or "done", and asks "Still playing?" when the inactivity check-in fires.
- [ ] **Waits only after handing over a turn**
  The assistant waits only when it has handed the player a turn. The rest of review mode is unchanged: while the assistant is analysing or playing both sides of a line itself, or while the two are only discussing a position, it does not wait.

## Constraints

- Must work on both ways the playmat runs today, the desktop server and the hosted multi-user server, including the hosted server's per-user games and idle expiry.
- Must reach the assistant only through the playmat's existing assistant tool connection. The assistant has no other channel to learn about board events.
- Must keep the playmat a playmat, not a rules engine: the waiting reports what happened on the board, and the assistant still judges legality and chooses which Pokémon to promote.
- Any single call the assistant makes to wait must return within client and network timeouts (under a minute), so no call can hold the game or a connection open indefinitely.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] **Turn passes without a chat message**
  In a play-mode game, after the assistant ends its turn and the player then ends their turn on the board, the assistant starts its next turn without the player typing anything in chat.
- [ ] **Drill turn needs no "done" message**
  In review mode, when the assistant sets a replay drill and the player plays the turn on the board and ends it, the assistant moves on to comparing the player's line without the player saying in chat that they are done.
- [ ] **Played-out line alternates without chat**
  In review mode, while playing a line out with the player playing one seat and the assistant the other, each time the player ends their seat's turn on the board, the assistant plays the other seat's reply without a chat message.
- [ ] **Only the handed-over seat's turn ends the wait**
  In review mode, while the assistant is waiting on the seat whose turn it handed to the player, a turn ending for the other seat does not end the wait.
- [ ] **Hand-off says what the player did**
  When the player's turn ends, the result the assistant receives names the new turn and the seat whose turn it is, and includes the actions the player took during their turn, or a way to read exactly those actions.
- [ ] **Promotion after a mid-turn knockout**
  When the player knocks out the assistant's Active Pokémon with an attack, an ability or another effect, and the assistant has a Benched Pokémon, the assistant promotes a Benched Pokémon to the Active Spot while it is still the player's turn.
- [ ] **Judge makes the assistant shuffle and draw**
  When the player plays Judge on their turn, the assistant shuffles its own hand into its deck and draws 4 cards during the player's turn, without any chat message from the player.
- [ ] **Unfair Stamp makes the assistant shuffle and draw**
  When the player plays Unfair Stamp on their turn, the assistant shuffles its own hand into its deck and draws 2 cards during the player's turn, without any chat message from the player.
- [ ] **Cards with nothing for the assistant to do**
  When the player plays a card whose text gives the assistant's side nothing to do, the assistant makes no move and carries on waiting.
- [ ] **Board is locked during a mid-turn response**
  While the assistant is promoting or carrying out its part of an effect during the player's turn, game actions the player tries on the board are refused, and the board shows that the opponent is acting. Once the assistant finishes, the player's actions work again in the same turn.
- [ ] **Board is locked during the assistant's turn**
  During the assistant's own turn, game actions the player tries on the board are refused, and the board shows that the opponent is acting.
- [ ] **A stuck lock releases itself**
  If the assistant stops part-way through a mid-turn response or its turn (for example, the session is closed), the board unlocks within 2 minutes of the assistant's last move, and shows that the opponent stopped responding. A turn in which the assistant makes a move at least every minute stays locked until the assistant finishes.
- [ ] **Player keeps playing after a mid-turn response**
  After the assistant promotes or carries out its part of an effect mid-turn, the player can keep taking actions in the same turn. When the player then ends the turn, the assistant takes its turn as usual, without a chat message.
- [ ] **Quiet while the player is idle**
  With the player idle on their turn for under five minutes, the assistant posts nothing in chat and makes no game move.
- [ ] **Check-in after five idle minutes**
  If the player takes no board action for five minutes during their turn, the assistant stops waiting, and its next chat message is "Still playing?". It makes no other game move.
- [ ] **Active turn is not interrupted**
  If the player takes a board action at least once every 4 minutes over a 10-minute turn, the assistant never asks "Still playing?" during that turn.
- [ ] **Answering the check-in resumes the wait**
  After "Still playing?", if the player replies in chat and then ends their turn on the board, the assistant takes its turn without another chat prompt. If the player ended their turn before replying, the assistant takes its turn straight after the reply.
- [ ] **Game changes end the wait**
  While the assistant is waiting, any of these ends the wait within 60 seconds: switching the game's mode, opening another game, closing the game, the game expiring on the hosted server, switching to another branch, or rewinding. The result says which of these happened, and the assistant stops waiting.
- [ ] **Board shows "Opponent is waiting"**
  While the assistant is waiting on the player's turn, the board shows "Opponent is waiting". It disappears within 60 seconds once the assistant stops waiting, including after the "Still playing?" check-in.
- [ ] **Assistant follows the loop in a fresh session**
  In a new session, with no instruction from the player beyond starting a play-mode game or a replay drill, the assistant waits for the player after handing over the turn, and never asks the player to say "your turn" or "done".
- [ ] **No waiting or locking outside handed-over turns**
  In review mode, while the assistant is reviewing a turn, playing both sides of a line itself, or discussing a position, it does not wait, the board does not show "Opponent is waiting", and the player's board actions are never refused.
- [ ] **An idle hosted game still expires**
  On the hosted server, a game left with the assistant waiting and no player activity expires after the same idle time as a game with no assistant attached.

## Technical Approach

- Prefer a new long-running assistant tool that blocks until the player finishes or something needs the assistant, rather than any form of push from the playmat.
- Prefer a long-poll shape: each call is capped at around 30 s and reports "still waiting" when the cap is reached, and the assistant calls again. The five-minute inactivity check is tracked across calls rather than within one.
- Prefer having the wait report each card or effect the player uses, rather than the playmat keeping a list of cards that need a response.
- Prefer a whole-board lock over finer-grained rules about which actions may overlap.
- Prefer keeping the timing values (per-call cap around 30 s, inactivity check five minutes, lock release two minutes) as adjustable settings with these defaults, since they are product choices, not Pokémon rules.
- Prefer building the hand-off on the "your turn" message the board already produces when the player ends a turn in play mode, so play mode, drills and played-out lines share one hand-off.
- Teach the assistant the loop through the server's standing instructions plus the existing coaching guides (the replay drill and game review guides gain the waiting step), with a new guide for playing a game if the loop needs more room than a line or two.
- Risk: waits that end early (time-out, game swap, closed connection) can leave listeners or state behind on the game; left unchecked, abandoned waits pile up over a long session or on the hosted server.

## Success Metrics

- A full play-mode game against the assistant can be played from first turn to last with zero "your turn" (or "done") chat messages from the player.
- In a replay drill session, the player never has to tell the assistant in chat that they have finished playing a drill turn on the board.
- The assistant picks up its turn within a few seconds of the player ending theirs on the board, never after a delay of tens of seconds.
- Over a long session, including the player walking away and returning after "Still playing?", the playmat's memory and open connections do not grow with the number of waits made.

## Non-Goals

- No turn timer against the player: the five-minute check only pauses the assistant and asks "Still playing?". It never ends the player's turn, forfeits the game or takes any move on the player's behalf.
