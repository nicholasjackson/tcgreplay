# Wait for the player's turn (2026-10-26)

## What was built

The assistant can now wait on the playmat while the player takes a turn on the board, and pick up its own part by itself. That part is its next turn, a promotion after a knockout, or its side of a card such as Judge. The player no longer types "your turn" or "done" in chat.

- **A long-polling `wait_for_turn` tool.** The assistant calls it after handing the player a turn: after its own `end_turn` in play mode, or after setting a drill or a line to play out in review mode. Each call returns within the per-call cap (30 s by default, at most 50 s) with one outcome, and each outcome carries a `next` line saying exactly what to do:
  - `turn_ended`, with the new turn, whose turn it is, the hand-off prompt and the player's moves;
  - `respond`, with each card, Ability or attack the player used, plus its text, for the assistant to judge;
  - `promote`, when the assistant's Active Spot is empty;
  - `check_in` after five idle minutes, when the assistant asks exactly "Still playing?";
  - `ended`, with the reason (mode switched, game replaced, closed or expired, branch switched, rewound);
  - `still_waiting`.
- **A per-game-manager "presence"** holds the single wait session, the player's last board activity and the assistant's acting lock. It wakes waiting calls through a channel it replaces on every change, so waits register nothing that outlives them. A test shows listeners and goroutines return to baseline after hundreds of waits.
- **A response window** lets the assistant's seat act during the player's turn in play mode, only while a `respond`/`promote` outcome is open. In play mode the assistant (the opponent seat) can no longer slip into the player's turn by joining an action the player left open.
- **The acting lock on the board.** While the assistant answers a card, promotes or plays its own turn, the board refuses the player's game actions with "the opponent is acting; wait for them to finish", but still allows reads, replays, the reveal toggle and switching mode. The lock releases when the assistant waits again or ends its turn. After two minutes without an assistant move it releases itself and shows that the opponent stopped responding.
- **The board shows the opponent status:** "Opponent is waiting", "Opponent is acting" or "Opponent stopped responding" beside the opponent's name. It disables its controls while the opponent acts, and stops asking the player to paste a prompt when the assistant is already waiting.
- **Hosted:** a wait finds the account's game without counting as activity, so an abandoned game still expires on schedule, and the expiry ends the wait.
- **Settings:** the per-call cap, the check-in time and the lock-release time are adjustable with `-wait-cap`/`TCGREPLAY_WAIT_CAP`, `-check-in`/`TCGREPLAY_CHECK_IN` and `-lock-release`/`TCGREPLAY_LOCK_RELEASE`, in every run mode. Bad values stop start-up with a clear message.
- **Teaching the loop:** the server's standing instructions gain the play-loop line. The replay-drill and review-game guides gain the waiting step wherever they hand the player a turn.
- **Game change notices** now say what kind of change happened (mode, checkout, rewind, undo), a mode switch now notifies open boards, and the "Your turn in tcgreplay …" hand-off text is built in one place for the board and the wait.

## Why it matters

Players practising against the assistant get a match that flows like a real one: no chat message to pass the turn, the assistant answering Judge or a knockout mid-turn by itself, and a board that shows what the opponent is doing. This works in play mode, in replay drills and in played-out lines, on both the desktop and the hosted playmat. A simple "Still playing?" check covers a player who steps away.

## Deviations from the plan

- `Game.HandOff` takes the `since` cursor rather than a seat.
- **Fresh waits:** a fresh wait in review mode during the other seat's turn waits for the handed-over seat's next turn. In play mode it is accepted during the assistant's turn only while the player's turn end is still unanswered (nothing done since).
- **Cursor on mid-turn outcomes:** `respond` and `promote` keep the cursor at the turn's start, and reported cards are de-duplicated by action id. The final hand-off therefore still covers the whole turn.
- **Logs on every outcome:** every outcome carries the player's moves as log lines, except an `ended` wait whose moves are no longer on the board's line.
- **The waiting grace** (20 s, keeping "waiting" shown between polls) was implemented with the acting lock rather than with the first presence task.
- **The lock guard** on the board is one allow-list check (`lookVerbs`).
- **Validation:** timing validation runs in `main` for every run mode.
- **Start-up log:** the start-up log names the effective timings.
- **User decision during implementation:** the assistant plays the opponent seat, so in play mode its cross-seat joins onto the player's open action also need the response window. The player's side of the assistant's cards still joins as before.
- **Goal-check fixes:**
  - The `ended` outcome gained its log.
  - The cross-seat join gap was closed.
  - The guide test anchors were tightened to each step's own sentence.
- **Optional test not added:** the end-to-end hosted HTTP test of a waiting assistant; registry-level tests cover it.
- **Test files:** several tests live in new files (`wait.go`/`wait_test.go`, `presence_*_test.go`, `lock_test.go`, `timing_test.go`) rather than the files the plan named.
