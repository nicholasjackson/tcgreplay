# Touch-friendly board

## What was built

The web board now works by touch on an iPad and an iPhone, and mouse use on the desktop keeps working as before. All the work is in the `tcgreplay` repo, in the Svelte board under `web/`, plus Go test support. The board server, the MCP tools and the game model are unchanged.

- **One input path for dragging.** A single Pointer Events controller (`web/src/lib/pointer.svelte.js`) replaces HTML5 drag and drop. Mouse, pen and touch all drag cards and Pokémon through it to every place a mouse could drop them before, with the same result:
  - while dragging, a ghost follows the finger, every drop place is outlined, and the one underneath is lit;
  - a miss changes nothing;
  - in the hand, a sideways swipe scrolls and an upward drag lifts a card.
- **One action sheet, three containers.** The menus are grouped in the design's order (attacks, abilities, moving, damage, conditions, other, with Knock Out last). One `ActionSheet` renders them:
  - full screen on a phone;
  - as a panel with the card beside its actions on a tablet;
  - as a popover at the pointer with a mouse.

  A tap opens a card's actions with the card and its text. With a mouse, a click still zooms and a right-click opens the popover. Damage is a stepper and Special Conditions are one-tap toggles.
- **Targets chosen on the board.** Attach, Evolve, Retreat, Switch and moving an attached card are one action each. Choosing one shows a banner naming the action, lights the candidates and waits for a tap; anything else cancels. One candidate acts at once and names it ("Retreat to Riolu"). Attaching an Energy to a benched Pokémon takes three taps.
- **Panels replace browser dialogs.** An attack opens a panel with its printed damage to adjust (with ×2 for Weakness) and a target that defaults to the opponent's Active and can be changed on the board. Its confirm button names the result ("Attack Riolu for 140"). Draw N and Look at top N pick a count capped at the cards left in the deck. Nothing on the board opens a browser dialog any more.
- **Touch-friendly controls.**
  - On touch, hover enlargement and the hover-only damage buttons are off, while the mouse keeps them.
  - On a touch screen, every control and card is at least 44×44 CSS px.
  - Cards suppress text selection and the iOS callout.
  - The timeline's rewind and play buttons show without a hover.
- **A compact phone board.** On a phone held upright:
  - each player gets a counter bar (Deck, Discard, Lost, Prizes, and the opponent's hand count) that opens the pile, the deck's actions or a new prize view;
  - the bench is one row scrolling to all 8 slots;
  - the hand is a strip of larger cards, and the middle strip is a single row;
  - the header keeps the connection dot, warnings and ☰, with the branch, Review mode, Reveal all and a full-screen timeline in ☰.

  Held sideways, a phone shows a notice to turn it upright.
- **The repo's first browser tests.** Playwright runs 203 tests from `make test`, after the Go checks, against a Go test server (`internal/webui/webtest`). That server serves the built board from a fixed review-mode position and resets it before each test. The profiles are an iPhone-sized and an iPad-sized touch screen and a desktop mouse, and any browser dialog fails a test. The fake card pool that three Go test packages had each copied now lives once in `internal/cards/cardstest`.

## Why it matters

Players can follow, play and review games from whatever device is in their hand. Before this, the board responded only to a mouse:
- cards couldn't be moved by touch;
- menus couldn't be opened, because they needed a right-click;
- numbers were entered in browser dialogs;
- the board didn't fit a phone.

One build now serves phones, tablets and desktops, on both the desktop app and the hosted playmat.

## Deviations from the plan

- **The touch test profiles run in Chromium's touch emulation, not WebKit** (the user's decision). Playwright's WebKit needs Ubuntu library versions and won't launch on the development machine. Touch drags are driven with real CDP touch input. Real iOS Safari behaviour is covered by the manual test plan.
- **The fake card pool lives in `internal/cards/cardstest` and serves all three Go test packages** (game, webui, usergames), not just the board server's tests.
- **Sheet and target details:**
  - The sheet holds a builder (`app.sheet.build`) that re-runs as the board changes, so damage and conditions stay live while it is open.
  - Taps are routed by the click's pointer type rather than by a callback from the drag controller.
  - Target requests carry a hint line and candidate names. A capture-phase click listener completes or cancels target mode.
- **The drag controller captures the pointer on the document, not the card, and hover enlargement is off while dragging.** Tests found a hover-enlarged card stealing drops.
- **The deck's sheet shows the deck as a card back with a short text.** The goal check found the deck showed no card.
- **Pile views on touch use different previews than planned.** A tap on a discard, search or look card opens that card's sheet. A deck-list card opens the enlarged card. The desktop side preview follows the mouse only.
- **On touch, cards are at least 44px wide,** so Energy, Tools and prizes are larger on a tablet than with a mouse.
- **"Change ›" in the attack panel hides the sheet, which keeps the panel's state, while the target is tapped.**
- **The phone board scrolls vertically.** Only sideways scrolling is ruled out.
- **The "phone held sideways" check is a coarse pointer under 500px tall,** so a short tablet window never shows the notice.
- **Cards in pile views keep vertical scrolling,** so a card is dragged out of a view by starting sideways.
